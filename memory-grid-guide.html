Memory Grid: How to Play the Tile Memory Game

Memory Grid shows a group of glowing tiles on a three-by-three board. After a short display, the highlights disappear. Your task is to select the positions that were lit. Complete the pattern to move to a harder round.

How a round works
  1. Watch the board while the highlighted tiles are visible.
  2. Wait for the board to switch to selection mode.
  3. Tap the positions you remember.
  4. Find every highlighted tile to advance to the next pattern.

Look for a pattern, not just separate squares

Instead of trying to hold each tile as an unrelated detail, notice how the positions relate to one another. For example, you might remember a pair along the top row and another tile near a corner. Describing a small shape to yourself can make the display easier to revisit mentally.

Use a consistent way to scan the board

Choose a simple scanning order, such as left to right and top to bottom. A consistent order can help you keep track of where each highlight appeared. You can also divide the board into three rows or four corner-and-center areas. Use whichever description feels natural.

Do not rush the selection phase

Once the highlights disappear, take a brief moment to recall the positions before tapping. Selecting a tile removes the need to remember that position again. If you are unsure, make your best choice and continue calmly rather than tapping randomly.

What happens when you make a mistake?

An incorrect selection reduces your current score by one point, but the round continues. Correct selections add points. The game becomes more demanding as you complete patterns and advance through levels, so it is normal for accuracy to change as you progress.

A small example of a visual pattern

Imagine that the glowing tiles appear in the top-left corner, the top-middle position, and the bottom-right corner. You might remember them as “two across the top, then one at the opposite corner.” Turning positions into a short description can be easier than trying to repeat a list of unrelated squares.

What if you forget one position?

Pause briefly when the selection phase begins and recall the pattern you noticed. If you are uncertain, make a considered choice rather than tapping several tiles quickly. An incorrect selection affects the score, but it is part of the game and does not mean you need to restart immediately.

Keep the goal realistic

Memory Grid is a casual browser game. It can give you a short opportunity to practice noticing and recalling a visual arrangement, but a score in the game is not a clinical measure of memory and does not prove that your memory has improved.
